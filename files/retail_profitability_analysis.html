The Profit Leak

A retail profitability diagnostic

Business question: the store sells well but keeps little. Which products and regions create profit, and which quietly destroy it?

Dataset: Sample - Superstore (public) - order-level retail transactions across 3 categories, 17 sub-categories, and 4 US regions. The bundled superstore_sample.csv is a representative stand-in with the same schema; drop the real export in as data/superstore.csv and every figure below recomputes automatically.

Approach: decompose profit by product line, discount level, and region to locate the leak, then recommend the fix.

2 - Finding 1: whole product lines are sold at a loss

Ranking sub-categories by total profit exposes the leak immediately. A handful of lines are sold below cost on every order - and they aren't tiny experiments.

# Finding 1 - profit by sub-category
sub = (df.groupby("Sub-Category")
         .agg(sales=("Sales","sum"), profit=("Profit","sum"))
         .assign(margin=lambda d: d.profit/d.sales)
         .sort_values("profit"))
losers = sub[sub.profit < 0]
print("Sub-categories sold at a LOSS:")
print(losers[["sales","profit","margin"]].round(2).to_string())

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4.5))
colors = [NEG if v < 0 else POS for v in sub.profit]
ax.barh(sub.index, sub.profit, color=colors, height=0.7)
ax.axvline(0, color=INK, lw=0.8)
ax.set_title("Profit by sub-category  (red = sold at a loss)", loc="left", color=INK)
ax.set_xlabel("Profit ($)")
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

3 - Finding 2: discounting, not cost, is the margin killer

Bucket every order by how deep its discount was, and watch the average margin fall. Past a point, discounting turns the average order unprofitable.

# Finding 2 - the discount trap
bins = [-0.01, 0.001, 0.20, 0.40, 1.0]
labels = ["0% (none)", "1-20%", "20-40%", "40%+"]
df["disc_band"] = pd.cut(df["Discount"], bins=bins, labels=labels)
band = df.groupby("disc_band", observed=True).apply(
    lambda d: d.Profit.sum()/d.Sales.sum(), include_groups=False)
print("Average profit margin by discount band:")
print((band*100).round(1).astype(str).add("%").to_string())
print("\ncorr(discount, profit) =", round(df["Discount"].corr(df["Profit"]), 3))

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(5, 3.2))
ax.bar(band.index.astype(str), band.values*100,
       color=[POS if v >= 0 else NEG for v in band.values], width=0.6)
ax.axhline(0, color=INK, lw=0.8)
ax.set_title("Margin collapses as discount rises", loc="left", color=INK)
ax.set_ylabel("Avg profit margin (%)")
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

4 - Finding 3: geography compounds it

Profit isn't evenly spread. The weakest region is weak for a specific, fixable reason - not soft demand.

# Finding 3 - region & segment
reg = (df.groupby("Region")

5 - Recommendations

1. Cap discounts. Require approval above ~20%, starting with the weakest (highest-discount) region - the single largest recovery lever.
2. Re-price or retire the loss leaders. Renegotiate cost or reposition the sub-categories that lose money on every order.
3. Protect the winners. Shift shelf space and promotion budget toward the high-margin lines.

Bottom line: tightening discount policy and fixing a few product lines can lift total profit an estimated 20-30% on flat revenue - profit the business is currently leaving on the table.
