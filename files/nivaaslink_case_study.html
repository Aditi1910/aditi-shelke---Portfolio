01 · The business problem

A pool of below-market homes that everyday buyers can't reach.

Every year Indian banks auction thousands of repossessed properties, often well below market price. The catch: those listings are scattered across dozens of bank websites in inconsistent formats, wrapped in legal complexity, and effectively invisible to an ordinary buyer.

The people who'd benefit most — middle-income families looking for an affordable home — are the least equipped to find, evaluate, and act on them. So the discount flows almost entirely to investors and insiders, and a real housing-affordability lever goes unused.

02 · Background

No product yet — just a question worth answering.

NivaasLink was an early-stage startup with nothing built. The founding question wasn't "can we ship an app," it was "is the need real, and what would it actually take to serve it?" My job was the research and analysis that answered that before engineering spent a rupee — the classic pre-build discovery a business analyst owns.

03 · Research questions

What we needed to know first.

  • Q1How large and how real is the discount on bank auction properties?
  • Q2Why don't middle-income buyers access these deals today?
  • Q3What would a buyer actually need to act with confidence?
  • Q4Is there a defensible gap that no existing platform fills?
04 · Stakeholders

A two-sided market, four interests to balance.

05 · Approach & method

Let real listings answer the question.

Rather than assume, I built the evidence base. I scraped and structured 50 live auction listings across 8 Indian cities from bank sources, benchmarked each against market value to quantify the discount, then audited existing platforms for the buyer support they actually provide. Barriers became requirements; requirements became the product.

Frameworks: competitive & gap analysis · value proposition / jobs-to-be-done · market sizing · requirements definition.

06 · What the analysis found

The barrier wasn't price. It was trust and guidance.

41.8%
average discount below market across the 50-listing sample
95%
of retail buyers never access these deals
0 / 50
listings on existing platforms offered any buyer guidance

The discount was real and large — but it wasn't the problem. Supply exists; the gap is that no one helps an ordinary buyer navigate fragmentation, legal risk, and financing. That reframed NivaasLink from "another listings site" into a guidance-and-trust product.

07 · The product

Five features, each removing one barrier.

Listing aggregation
fixes  fragmentation across dozens of bank sites
Price comparison
fixes  the discount is invisible without a market benchmark
AI risk indicators
fixes  legal & title opacity that scares buyers off
Loan matching
fixes  financing uncertainty for first-time buyers
Step-by-step guidance
fixes  the missing hand-holding — the wedge no rival offers
08 · Recommendations

Build the guidance, not just the list.

  1. Lead with an information product, not a listings feed. Guidance is the differentiator competitors leave on the table.
  2. Prioritise trust and risk features first. The real barrier is confidence, not access — de-risking the purchase is the unlock.
  3. Launch in the 8 cities with proven inventory, then scale nationally once the guidance model is validated.
09 · Potential impact

A business case, and a fairer market.

Projected 3-year ARR growth from ₹24L at MVP to ₹348L at national scale — and, beyond the P&L, below-market housing access opened up for a segment the current market quietly shuts out.

Figures are forward projections from the research model, not realised revenue.

10 · What I took from it

The data reframed the problem.

I went in expecting the story to be about price. The listings said otherwise: the bottleneck was trust and guidance, not supply or discount. Letting real, scraped data challenge the founding assumption — instead of validating it — is the part I'm proudest of, and the habit I bring to every project since.
